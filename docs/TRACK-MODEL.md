# FREQUENCY — how a track is built

Hand this to an AI, or to a person. It is the whole model in one page.

## The seven layers

A track is seven layers. Each one is EARNED by flying, never given, and each
has two levels: `EARNED` when you win it, `DEEP` once a second voice has grown
onto it (§32).

| layer | what it is | its second voice at DEEP | what you see |
|---|---|---|---|
| KICK | the foundation | broken percussion on its own cycle | shockwave through the ground |
| SNARE | the backbeat (clap or snare) | a second machine a hair late | flash across the field |
| HATS | the top end | 32nds of dirt above it | particle sparkle |
| BASS | the low end | the sub stays UNDER it | wide, slow terrain deformation |
| HARMONY | chords, from your resonances | a wide voice an octave up | bridges between things |
| MELODY | a phrase, from your pitch path | the phrase an octave up, slower | a light trail behind you |
| TEXTURE | air, noise, room | a second, contrasting texture | environmental particles |

An eighth layer, ATMOSPHERE, is never earned: it is the bed under everything.

## The order is the genre

Every world builds in its own order (`GenreLadder`), and only the NEXT unearned
rung can be won — deliberate play brings it forward, never skips it.

    techno     kick → hats → snare → bass → harmony → melody → texture
    garage     hats → kick → snare → bass → harmony → melody → texture
    jazz       harmony → bass → hats → kick → melody → snare → texture
    ambient    texture → harmony → bass → melody → hats → kick (last!)
    trap       kick → hats → bass → snare → melody → harmony → texture
    bass music kick → bass → snare → hats → melody → harmony → texture

## Four things shape those layers

1. **GRAMMAR (where you are).** The world decides the figure, the drum machine,
   the instruments, the tempo and the mix. Same layer, different music.
2. **SECTION (the arrangement).** Sections add and REMOVE parts, not just
   volume: intro = kick + air, build = the bass is gone, drop = it all returns,
   break = drums and bass out. Each family means this differently — ambient
   swells instead of dropping, dub lets the echo answer.
3. **PERFORMANCE (how you fly).** Altitude is a tape: up is faster and higher,
   down by the ground is slower, deeper, darker and heavier. Wind is loudness
   and note length. Dissonance is edge.
4. **VARIATION.** Every section change rewrites one layer with a different
   transform of the same part, seeded so a flight is reproducible.

## What the controls mean

- **Direction** chooses the world. Ten of them, 36° apart. A track keeps the
  grammar it was born in; flying somewhere else starts a NEW track there.
- **Speed** is not tempo. It is ENERGY and DEVELOPMENT: it decides how fast
  layers are earned, how fast sections move, and how each world spends it
  (trap subdivides its hats, techno adds voices, ambient adds air, never drums).
- **Altitude** is pitch and tempo together, and nothing else.
- **Wind (LMB)** is force: loudness, note length, and the throttle.
- **Region tempo** is fixed per world; your own tapped rhythm overrides it.

## The two rules that never bend

- **Movement is the music.** Stand still and the track goes silent — but every
  earned layer stays earned (§42).
- **What you see is what you hear.** Every visual event is a real note read off
  the pattern that is about to play, never a guess from the beat counter (§63).
